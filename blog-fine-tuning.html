Fine-Tuning LLMs: When It Helps, When It Doesn't & How to Do It Right

Most teams reach for fine-tuning too early. Prompt engineering and RAG solve 80% of cases at a fraction of the cost. But when fine-tuning is the right tool, it dramatically outperforms alternatives — here's how to know which situation you're in.

Fine-tuning excels at style, format, and behavior adaptation. It fails at injecting new factual knowledge reliably. Understanding that distinction saves months of wasted work.

When fine-tuning genuinely wins

✅ Consistent output format at high volume

If you need 100K+ calls/day all returning the same JSON schema, fine-tuning is cheaper than paying extra tokens for format instructions in every prompt — the format becomes implicit at inference time.

✅ Domain-specific tone and vocabulary

Legal, medical, or brand-specific language that's hard to specify in a prompt. Fine-tuned models internalize tone. A RAG system still uses the base model's voice — fine-tuning changes the voice itself.

✅ Latency-sensitive applications

Fine-tuned smaller models (7B–13B) often match a prompted larger model for narrow tasks at 5–10× lower latency and cost. This math works for well-defined, repetitive tasks.

When fine-tuning doesn't help

❌ Injecting new factual knowledge

Fine-tuning on facts produces confident hallucinations, not reliable recall. Use RAG to inject knowledge. Fine-tune only for behavior and format — never for facts.

❌ Fewer than 500 examples

With under 500 high-quality examples, few-shot prompting almost always outperforms fine-tuning. The ROI crossover is around 500–1,000 quality examples.
