Comparing GPU cloud hosting for always-on AI agents

An AI agent that works around the clock needs a GPU that stays up, or a fair share of one. In 2026 there are many options for different needs and budgets, so it pays to compare prices by GPU model, priority tier and date. This guide compares five GPU clouds, Vast.ai, RunPod, Salad, Vagon and CoreWeave, and what each one costs for running AI agents 24/7.

How we checked: we read each provider's public pricing page or public API on 5 Nov 2026 between 16:05 and 16:25 UTC. Vast.ai's price list was last updated at 15:30 UTC. All prices are in US dollars per GPU per hour. A 24/7 month is 730 hours.

1. Vast.ai: a low-cost GPU marketplace

Vast.ai is a marketplace where independent hosts rent out their GPUs and set their own prices. On 5 Nov 2026, 77 GPU models had live offers in its price list, from consumer RTX cards to data-center H100s.

Pricing: On 5 Nov 2026 (15:30 UTC), Vast.ai's live price list showed H100 SXM rentals from USD 1.87 an hour on demand (median USD 2.39, all hosts) and RTX 3090s from USD 0.09 an hour. Interruptible H100 bids started at about USD 0.67-1.00 an hour (16:08-16:21 UTC, Vast.ai console). Prices move within minutes, so compare the same GPU model and priority tier.

For an agent that must stay up, look at verified hosts. In our two checks, at 16:08 and 16:21 UTC, only 2-4 verified machines offered H100 SXMs, and their cheapest price moved between USD 2.28 and 3.81 an hour. Verified RTX 3090s started at USD 0.13-0.15 an hour.

Pros:

  • Some of the lowest on-demand prices in this guide, such as RTX 3090s from USD 0.09 an hour.
  • A wide choice of GPUs, hosts and locations.
  • A public API for automated workflows. We used it for these price checks.

Cons:

  • Quality varies by host. The cheapest H100 SXM listing came from an unverified host.
  • Supply can be thin: only 6-8 machines offered H100 SXMs in our checks, so prices can jump within minutes.
  • Interruptible instances can stop at any time, so checkpoint your work.

Best for: budget-minded developers and experiments. For an always-on agent, choose a verified host and keep checkpoints.

2. RunPod: fixed hourly prices

RunPod rents GPUs at fixed list prices in two tiers: Community Cloud and the higher-priced Secure Cloud. Its pricing page cites GPUs in more than 30 regions.

Pricing: In November 2026 RunPod rented an RTX A5000 (24 GB) for USD 0.16 an hour and an H100 PCIe for USD 1.99 an hour on Community Cloud.

These are on-demand list prices, billed per second with no commitment, checked 5 Nov 2026 (RunPod's page is dated 4 Nov 2026). On Secure Cloud the same GPUs cost USD 0.27 and USD 2.89 an hour. An RTX 3090 costs USD 0.22 an hour on Community Cloud.

Pros:

  • Fixed, published prices that do not swing with a marketplace.
  • Per-second billing with no commitment.
  • Low prices for 24 GB cards, from USD 0.16 an hour for an RTX A5000.

Cons:

  • A list price is not a promise of stock. At 16:11 UTC on 5 Nov 2026, RunPod's public API showed no single-GPU stock for the RTX A5000, RTX 3090 or H100 PCIe.
  • Higher than the cheapest marketplace listings: an RTX 3090 costs USD 0.22 an hour, against USD 0.09 on Vast.ai.

Best for: steady agents that need a known monthly bill, such as one RTX A5000 for USD 116.80 a month. Check RunPod's terms for uptime commitments before you rely on it for production agents.

3. Salad: low-cost consumer GPUs

Salad runs your containers on idle consumer hardware, and you pick a priority tier for each job.

Pricing: Salad's lowest-priority batch tier starts at USD 0.015 an hour for RTX 3060-class consumer GPUs (November 2026). It suits batch work rather than always-on agents.

For an always-on agent, compare Salad's High priority tier, which other workloads never preempt. On 5 Nov 2026 it cost USD 0.20 an hour for an RTX 3090 and USD 0.44 for an RTX 4090. Billing is per second, and vCPU and RAM are included.

Pros:

  • Very low prices, from USD 0.015 per GPU-hour at Lowest priority.
  • Per-second billing, with vCPU and RAM included in the GPU price.
  • Four priority tiers, so you pay only for the reliability a job needs.

Cons:

  • Even High priority nodes can disconnect. Salad then moves your container to another node.
  • Medium, Low and Lowest tiers can be preempted by higher-priority work.

Best for: batch jobs and stateless inference behind a queue. For an always-on agent, use High priority and plan for restarts.

4. Vagon: GPU cloud desktops

Vagon rents cloud computers: a full desktop with a GPU that streams to your screen.

Pricing: Vagon's cloud computers start at USD 0.99 an hour for an NVIDIA T4 machine (4 cores, 16 GB RAM), billed per minute, plus a storage plan from USD 7.99 a month (5 Nov 2026).

The USD 7.99 plan includes 75 GB of disk. Prices vary by region: the same T4 machine costs USD 1.49 an hour in Hong Kong and USD 1.66 in São Paulo (Vagon region prices, 5 Nov 2026). Running it 24/7 would cost about USD 723 a month, plus storage.

Pros:

  • A full desktop with a GPU, streamed to your screen.
  • Per-minute billing for the time the computer runs.
  • Drag-and-drop file transfer, and machines with up to four GPUs.

Cons:

  • Costs more per hour than marketplace GPUs: USD 0.99 for a T4 machine, plus storage.
  • Built for hands-on desktop sessions rather than headless servers.

Best for: hands-on AI work in a full desktop, such as testing a model or preparing data, rather than agents that run 24/7.

5. CoreWeave: large GPU instances for companies

CoreWeave rents large GPU machines, most with 8 GPUs each, to companies that run AI at scale.

Pricing: CoreWeave lists an H100 at USD 6.16 an hour on demand (USD 2.46 spot) and an L40 at USD 1.25 an hour (November 2026).

These are per-GPU prices in North America on 5 Nov 2026. The H100 comes as an 8-GPU HGX instance at USD 49.24 an hour on demand (USD 19.71 spot), and the L40 as an 8-GPU instance at USD 10.00 an hour (USD 6.27 spot). Single-GPU prices are listed for its inference platform.

Pros:

  • Large machines: an HGX H100 instance has 8 GPUs with 80 GB each, 128 vCPUs and 2,048 GB of RAM.
  • Spot prices well below on-demand: USD 2.46 per H100-hour instead of USD 6.16.

Cons:

  • The highest on-demand H100 price in this guide.
  • You rent 8 GPUs at a time: an HGX H100 instance costs about USD 35,945 for a 24/7 month (our calculation).

Best for: companies running large training or inference jobs that need many GPUs in one place.

Final recommendations

When it comes to GPU cloud hosting for running AI agents 24/7 in 2026, the best choice depends on your specific use case:

  • Lowest cost, with some care: Vast.ai, if you pick verified hosts and checkpoint your work, or Salad's batch tier for work that can wait.
  • A steady, known bill: RunPod's fixed prices, such as USD 0.16 an hour for an RTX A5000 on Community Cloud.
  • Hands-on desktop work: Vagon's cloud computers, from USD 0.99 an hour for a T4 machine.
  • Large fleets: CoreWeave's 8-GPU instances, from USD 1.25 per L40-hour.
